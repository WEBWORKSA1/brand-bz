Naming

How to Name a Business: A 7-Step Framework That Works

By the Brand.bz Editorial Team · 10 min read · Updated November 06, 2026

A name is the most-used asset your brand will ever have. It appears in every conversation, search, invoice and referral. Changing it later is expensive, so a disciplined process up front pays for itself many times over.

Step 1 — Write a naming brief

Before brainstorming, answer five questions on one page: What do we do? Who is it for? What three feelings should the name evoke? Who are the main competitors and what are they called? Where will we operate (local, national, global)? The brief keeps the team from arguing over taste and lets you judge ideas against criteria.

Step 2 — Understand the main name types

TypeHow it worksTrade-off
DescriptiveSays what you do ("Fresh Bakes")Clear, but hard to protect and easy to copy
SuggestiveHints at a benefit or feelingGood balance of meaning and distinctiveness
CompoundTwo words fused togetherMemorable; check that the join reads cleanly
InventedA new word with no prior meaningHighly protectable; needs more marketing to build meaning
Founder / personalUses a person's namePersonal and trustworthy; harder to sell the business later
AcronymInitialsShort, but usually forgettable for new brands

Step 3 — Brainstorm wide

Aim for at least 100 candidates before judging any. Useful techniques:

  • Word banks: list words for your product, benefits, feelings, places and metaphors.
  • Blending: merge the start of one word with the end of another.
  • Affixes: add short prefixes or suffixes ("-ly", "-ify", "-hub").
  • Other languages: explore Latin, Greek or the languages of your market — and verify meanings with native speakers.
  • Generators: use our Brand Name Generator to produce dozens of combinations in seconds.

Step 4 — Screen with the five tests

  1. Radio test: say it out loud once. Can someone spell it correctly?
  2. Length test: two to three syllables and under ten letters is ideal.
  3. Meaning test: does it have negative meanings in key languages or slang?
  4. Distinctiveness test: is it clearly different from competitors?
  5. Stretch test: will it still fit if you add products or enter new markets?

Step 5 — Check domains and social handles

An exact-match .com is still the most trusted, but strong alternatives exist: short country codes, industry extensions, or adding a verb ("get", "try"). Secure matching handles on the social platforms your customers actually use on the same day.

Step 6 — Run a trademark knock-out search

Search official databases for identical and similar marks in your class of goods or services: the USPTO (United States), CIPO (Canada), EUIPO (European Union) and WIPO's Global Brand Database (international). A clean search is not a guarantee — a trademark professional can run a full clearance — but it filters out obvious conflicts early. Read our trademark basics guide.

Step 7 — Test with real people

Show your top three names to people in your target audience. Ask what kind of business each sounds like and which they would remember tomorrow. Then call them the next day and ask which ones they recall. Memory is the real test.

Shortcut: if you are torn between two finalists, pick the one that is easier to say over the phone. Word of mouth is still the cheapest marketing there is.
