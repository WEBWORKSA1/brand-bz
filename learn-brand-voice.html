Brand Voice: How to Sound Like Yourself Everywhere

By the Brand.bz Editorial Team · 7 min read · Updated November 06, 2026

If your visual identity is how your brand looks, your voice is how it sounds. A consistent voice makes a five-person company feel established and makes a large company feel human.

Voice vs. tone

Voice is your consistent personality. Tone is how that personality adapts to the situation. You speak differently at a celebration and at a funeral, but you are still you. Likewise, a brand might be playful on social media and calm and clear in a billing error message.

Step 1 — Choose three voice traits

Pick three adjectives that describe how you want to sound — for example confident, warm, plain-spoken. Three is enough to be distinctive and few enough to remember.

Step 2 — Define each trait with do's and don'ts

TraitWe areWe are not
ConfidentDirect, decisive, specificArrogant or dismissive
WarmFriendly, encouragingOverly casual or gushing
Plain-spokenShort sentences, everyday wordsJargon-heavy or vague

Step 3 — Write sample lines

Rewrite five common messages in your voice: a welcome email, an error message, a sale announcement, an apology and a social caption. These examples teach new team members faster than any rule.

Step 4 — Adjust tone by channel

  • Website: clear and benefit-led.
  • Social: more relaxed, more personality.
  • Support: calm, empathetic, solution first.
  • Sales proposals: confident, evidence-based.

Step 5 — Keep a word list

List words you always use (your product terms, signature phrases) and words you never use (jargon, competitor phrases, anything off-brand). Add it to your brand guide.

Quick check: cover your logo and read a piece of your content. Could it have been written by a competitor? If so, your voice needs more personality.
